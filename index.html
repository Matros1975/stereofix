MonoProfiles

Mono and balance for each sound output on your Mac — switched automatically.

How it works Questions Contact Privacy policy

How it works

Click the laptop icon in the menu bar. For any device — the built-in speakers, headphones, AirPods — switch on Use profile and choose Mono and Balance. Whenever your Mac switches to that output, MonoProfiles applies its profile. Devices without a profile keep macOS's normal behaviour.

Broken or crackling left speaker

Give the built-in speakers Mono on and Balance fully right: you hear both stereo channels from the working speaker, while headphones keep normal stereo.

Hearing in one ear

Turn on Mono just for your headphones; other outputs stay in stereo.

Questions

Why is everything still mono?

If System Settings › Accessibility › Audio › “Play stereo audio as mono” is on, macOS keeps every output in mono and MonoProfiles can't override it. Turn it off and let MonoProfiles handle mono per device. The app shows a notice when this is the case.

Why can't I change the balance for my USB interface or monitor?

Many USB interfaces, monitors and virtual devices have no balance control in macOS (the System Settings slider is greyed out too). For those, MonoProfiles offers Mono only.

Does mono affect other apps?

Yes. Mono is one system-wide macOS setting. MonoProfiles follows your current output, so an app that sends sound to another device at the same time gets the same mono setting.

What happens when I quit?

MonoProfiles gives macOS its own settings back: mono off (if MonoProfiles turned it on) and each device's original balance. Your profiles are kept for next time.

Does it process or record my audio?

No. It only switches macOS's own mono and balance settings. No audio processing, no drivers, no microphone access, no network access.
