One MacBook speaker crackling or dead? Hear everything from the good one

Short answer: turn on mono first, then move balance fully toward the working speaker. Mono mixes the left and right channels together, so the good speaker plays all of the sound. Moving balance alone, the usual advice, silences the broken side but also throws away everything recorded only on that channel.

macOS applies both settings to every output, so your headphones also become mono and one-sided. StereoFix, a free Mac app, applies mono and balance to the built-in speakers only and switches back automatically when you plug in headphones.

1. Find out which speaker is the problem

  1. Play music with a steady sound: a song or a podcast.
  2. Open System Settings › Sound › Output and select MacBook Pro Speakers (or MacBook Air Speakers).
  3. Drag Balance fully left, listen, then fully right, listen.
  4. If the crackle comes from one side only, that speaker is likely damaged. If it crackles on both sides, or also in wired headphones, the cause is more likely software: see rule out software first.

2. Rule out software first

  • Restart the Mac. If the crackle is gone, it was the audio service, not the speaker.
  • In Audio MIDI Setup (Applications › Utilities), select the speakers and try a different format, such as 44,100 or 48,000 Hz.
  • Quit apps that use a lot of CPU, and unplug USB audio devices and docks.
  • Run Apple Diagnostics: shut down, then hold the power button until startup options appear, and press Command-D.

If one side still crackles, the speaker is worn or torn. Apple or an authorised service provider can replace it. Until then, or if a repair isn't worth it, use the fix below.

3. Play all of the sound from the good speaker (manual fix)

  1. Open System Settings › Accessibility › Audio and turn on Play stereo audio as mono.
  2. Open System Settings › Sound › Output, select the built-in speakers and drag Balance fully toward the working speaker.

Both stereo channels now come out of the good speaker. We verified this with separate left and right test tones on a MacBook Pro: with mono on and balance fully right, both tones play from the right speaker.

Why balance alone isn't enough

Stereo recordings put some sounds only on the left channel and others only on the right. Balance fully to one side mutes the other channel, so those sounds disappear: part of the band, one voice in a podcast, effects in a game. Mono first mixes both channels, so nothing is lost.

Automatic fix with StereoFix

  1. Install StereoFix from the Mac App Store (free) and click the laptop icon in the menu bar.
  2. Turn off “Play stereo audio as mono” in Accessibility › Audio: it forces mono on every output and overrides StereoFix.
  3. Under MacBook Pro Speakers, switch on Use profile, then Mono, and drag Balance toward the working speaker.

That's it. StereoFix needs no Accessibility, microphone or screen permission, collects no data, and only changes the macOS mono and balance settings when your output changes. Quitting it gives macOS its own settings back.

Frequently asked questions

Why does only one of my MacBook speakers crackle?
Usually the speaker itself is worn or its membrane is torn, often after loud playback. If the crackle moves with the Balance slider to one side only, it is the speaker; if it is on both sides or in headphones too, it is more likely software.
Can I keep using a MacBook with one broken speaker?
Yes. Turn on mono and move balance fully to the working speaker, and you hear all of the sound from that speaker. StereoFix does this for the built-in speakers only and leaves headphones in stereo.
Is moving the balance slider enough?
No. Balance alone mutes the other stereo channel, so sounds recorded only on that side disappear. Turn on mono first so both channels are mixed into the working speaker.
Will playing louder through one speaker damage it?
One speaker gives less volume than two, so people turn it up. Keep the volume moderate; a speaker driven hard for long periods wears faster.
Does StereoFix repair the speaker?
No. StereoFix doesn't process audio. It switches macOS's own mono and balance settings per output, so you can keep using the good speaker until you repair the broken one.
